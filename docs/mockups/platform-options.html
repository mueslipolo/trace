One platform, four ways not to overload it

Five services, and everything that watches and administers them. The same system drawn four ways, from the least lines to the most separate diagrams. None of this is built: pick the one that feels right, or tell me what to change, and only then do we look at Trace.

1 Arrows to the zone, one collector

Only seven cross-cutting arrows in the whole picture. The cluster zone is one end of every concern: secrets in, scrape in, and a collector inside it that sends logs and traces out. The five services are not tied to anything.

Says the truth at the level the reader needs (the cluster is observed, not each service). Costs: it cannot show that Catalog has no secrets; the collector is a node you must add.

Needs nothing new in Trace: links to a frame already work.

2 Coloured dots instead of lines

Not one cross-cutting line. Each service carries a dot for each concern it uses; each hub carries the dot of what it serves. The reader matches colours.

Scales to any number of services and concerns and cannot tangle. It shows who uses what (Catalog has no purple dot). It does not show direction, and the reader has to look up the colour.

Needs a node badge feature and a legend line.

3 A platform band, one connector

The shared services sit in one band under the cluster, as in an Azure or AWS reference diagram. One wide connector between the zone and the band says "the cluster uses all of this", with the concerns named on it.

The calmest picture and the usual one. Costs: no per-concern detail, and the connector is a statement, not a link, so it needs a special element.

Needs a "connector band" between two zones: one element, no routing.

4 Three views of one model

The same system as three small diagrams, as C4 does: what runs, what watches it, who may get in. Each is readable on its own and none carries the others' lines.

Never overloaded, by construction. Costs: the reader has to assemble the whole themselves, and the model must be able to produce a view from a subset.

Needs a "view" statement that picks what to show. The biggest change, and the one with the biggest effect.
