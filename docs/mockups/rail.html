Rail: five ways to draw it

The same diagram five times: a chain of firewalls and servers where three of them send logs to a SIEM. A rail is for links that touch many nodes and carry no part of the story, so the aim is the least ink that still says "these three feed that one". What the rail draws today is the first picture below.

Today

A grey band the width of the SIEM node with its title across the top, a bus line inside it, dashed links with a label each and a dot at every tap.

What is wrong: the band is a zone that is not a zone, the title is crossed by the first link, the label repeats, and the band is as heavy as the frames of the real network.

A thin bus

No band, no box. One light line beside the diagram with a dot at every tap and the SIEM node at its end. The label is written once, along the line.

Closest to how a hand draws a bus. Costs almost nothing in space and cannot be mistaken for a zone. The links are light dashed lines.

B slim lane, vertical title

A narrow grey lane on the edge, the title turned to read along it, the SIEM inside. Links stop at the lane border and continue inside as a short solid tap.

Keeps the idea of a zone (it is a network of its own) but takes 100px instead of a wide band. The vertical title is the only text on top of the lane.

C bus bar

The bus is a solid bar like the bus bars of an electrical drawing; the links simply end on it. No dots, no band. The word "logs" sits above the bar.

The strongest object: the eye finds it first. Good when several rails run side by side (they become parallel bars). A little heavier than A.

D tags instead of lines

No lines at all: each node that logs gets a small tag, and the SIEM stands apart with a note. The reader follows the tag, not a line.

Scales to twenty nodes with no clutter and no crossing, at the price of not seeing the path. Best for logs and metrics, the least good for traffic that matters.

E bus with ports

Like A, but each tap ends in a small square port on the line instead of a dot, and the label sits in a small tag beside the line.

The ports read as sockets, which suits network diagrams. More ink than A for the same meaning.
